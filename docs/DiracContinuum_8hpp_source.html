5/*!
6 @brief Exact relativistic continuum (en > 0) hydrogen-like (Coulomb)
7 wavefunctions
8
9 @details In the form
10 \f[
11 \psi_{\en\kappa m}(\vb{r}) = \frac{1}{r}
12 \begin{pmatrix} f_{\en\kappa}(r)\,\Omega_{\kappa m}(\hat n) \\
13 i\,g_{\en\kappa}(r)\,\Omega_{-\kappa,m}(\hat n) \end{pmatrix},
14 \f]
15
16 normalised on the energy scale,
17
18 \f[
19 \int_0^\infty (f_\en f_{\en'} + g_\en g_{\en'})\,dr
20 = \delta(\en - \en').
21 \f]
22
23 See Methods for full definitions.
24
25 \par Non-relativistic limit
26 Passing \f$ \alpha \le 0 \f$ selects the non-relativistic limit directly:
27 the large component reduces to the energy-normalised Coulomb function
28 (f = P_el, see @ref P_el), the small component vanishes (g = 0). This path
29 does not require FLINT.
30
31 @note Requires the FLINT library (for confluent hypergeometric functions of
32 complex argument); available at compile time via the constexpr flag
33 DiracContinuum::available. (Not required for the non-relativistic limit.)
34
35 @warning Without FLINT, calling f and g may abort, or return NaN.
36 Use @ref DiracContinuum::available to check if available.
37
38 The optional electron mass parameter @p m defaults to 1 (atomic units).
39 The full relativistic energy is E = m*c^2 + en = m/alpha^2 + en, with
40 en > 0 for continuum states.
41*/

55double pe(double en, double alpha, double m = 1.0);

57/*!
58 @brief Both radial components {f, g} at radius r.
59 @details More efficient than separate calls to f() and g(), since the
60 (expensive) hypergeometric factors are shared. For \f$ \alpha \le 0 \f$
61 returns the non-relativistic limit {P_el, 0} (see @ref P_el), which does not
62 require FLINT.
63 @return std::pair {f, g}
64 @note alpha <= 0 selects the non-relativistic limit: f = P_el, g = 0
65 @warning For alpha > 0: prints a warning and returns {NaN, NaN} if compiled
66 without FLINT (see DiracContinuum::available)
67*/
68std::pair<double, double> fg(double r, double en, int kappa, double zeff,
69 double alpha, double m = 1.0);

103/*!
